International Rules of Gomoku

Introduction

These official Gomoku rules (only “Rules” for future reference) have been issued by the Gomoku Committee of the Renju International Federation (only “RIF” for future reference) in order to generally define the rules and standards for playing Gomoku in all official competitions.

Obeying these rules is a requirement in order for a game to be eligible for the official RIF Gomoku rating. Additional rules can be set by an organizer of a competition.

Foreword

The Rules cannot cover all possible situations that might occur during a game. In the event that a situation arises which is not explicitly covered by any of the rules and articles, it will be necessary to come up with a correct decision by using analogies to the situations, which are covered. The Rules expect that the referee will apply the necessary skills, sound judgment and total objectivity.

Basic rules of the game

Article 1 — The Nature and the Aims of Gomoku

1.1Gomoku is a sports-like, logical game for two players, who alternately place stones of a black and white colour on any given, non-occupied intersections on the “Board”.

1.2The Board is a square-shaped grid of 15 horizontal and 15 vertical intersections, creating a grand total of 225 intersections.

1.3Alternatively, crosses and circles may be used instead of black and white stones. This would mean, that instead of a square-shaped grid with 225 intersections, a square-shaped grid with 225 vacant squares should be used, in accordance with the parameters stated in article 1.2.

1.4The player with black stones will begin (only “First player” for future reference).

1.5Players alternate in placing the stones. A player’s turn starts after his/her opponent has executed his/her Move.

1.6The aim of both players is to create an uninterrupted horizontal, vertical, or diagonal row of exactly 5 stones of their colour.

Article 2 — Swap 2 Opening

2.1Before the game starts it will be determined which player is the First player.

2.2The First player will place 3 stones on any given, vacant intersection in the following order: black, white, black.

2.3The opponent will then have the opportunity to:

2.3.1choose which colour he/she would like to play with, or

2.3.2add two more stones onto the Board in the following order: white, black, and then let first player finally decide which colour he/she would like to play with.

2.4After this, the player with white stones will place a stone and thereafter, they will alternate in placing stones until the end of the game.

Article 3 — Execution of a Move

3.1To execute a Move means to place a stone on any given vacant intersection on the Board.

3.2The opening (according to the article 2.2) and the choice of the colour or the addition of 2 stones (according to the article 2.3) are considered as a Move execution as well.

3.3In the event that a player discontinues the connection between a stone and his/her hand, he/she is no longer allowed to relocate it or remove it.

Article 4 — The End of a Game

4.1A player wins a game when he/she creates an uninterrupted row of exactly 5 stones of his/her colour, and notifies his/her opponent about this at which point, the game will immediately end.

4.2If a player creates an uninterrupted row of exactly 5 stones of their colour, but does not notify his/her opponent about the victory, the game will continue until the player notifies his/her opponent, or the game ends in another way.

4.3In case of the creation of an uninterrupted row of 6 or more stones of the same colour, the game will continue.

4.4If a player surrenders, his/her opponent will win and the game will immediately end at this point.

4.5The game will end immediately in a draw if all intersections on the Board are occupied.

4.6The game will end in a draw if there is an agreement between both players, and if both players have made at least one Move.

Tournament rules

Article 5 — Time Measurement

5.1In order to measure time, clock with two dials/displays, connected in a way, in which they can’t run simultaneously, are being used.

5.2During a game, each player, after executing his/her Move, will stop his/her clock and start the opponent’s clock. This signals the end of his/her Move.

5.3A player may stop his/her clock only after executing his/her Move.

5.4A player must press the clock with the same hand used to place the stone onto the Board.

5.5Only the player whose time is running is allowed to adjust the inaccurate position of stones on the Board.

5.6Each player must complete all of his/her Moves within the time limit, or defined additional time can be added for each Move made.

5.7Unless one of the following situations 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5, 4.6 happens:

5.7.1A game is won by the player who rightly makes notification that his/her opponent has used up all of his/her time. After this, the game immediately ends.

5.7.2A game immediately ends as a draw if both players have used all of their time and neither of them, before running out of time themselves, has claimed their victory in accordance to article 5.7.1.

5.8At the time determined for the game to begin, the starting player’s clock will be activated.

5.9If it is necessary to interrupt the game, the referee will pause both clocks. A player may only stop both clocks in a case where he/she needs the referee’s help to resolve an impropriety or the disobedience of the rules. The referee will decide when the game should continue. In the case of both clocks being stopped, the position on the desk must be covered.

Article 6 — Faults

6.1In the case where a stone or stones on the Board appear to be in a conflict with the rules, and a stone/stones must be returned to a previous position, the referee will edit the setting of the clock in accordance with his/her best awareness and conscience. He/she also has the option not to edit the time on the clock.

6.2In the case that a position has been destroyed due to a player’s fault and it is possible to rectify it, the position will be rectified while the clock is on and the time is running for the player who caused disruption. If it is not possible to rectify the position, the culprit will lose the game.

6.3In the case where it is not possible to rectify the situation but it is not the players’ fault, the game cannot be counted and it will be necessary to play the game again.

6.4In the case that during a game it is discovered that the setting of one or both of the clocks of the players is incorrect, the player or the referee is obliged to stop the clocks. The referee will then set them correctly and edit the time if necessary. The referee will edit the time in accordance with his/her best awareness and conscience.

Article 7 — Notation of the Moves

7.1If the tournament rules stipulate so, each player is required to annotate all of the Moves made on the Board, Move after Move, into the Gomoku notation. This is usually done on a piece of paper as precisely and legibly as possible.

7.2A player must record all previous Moves made on the Board before he/she executes his/her Move. It is not allowed to annotate Moves in advance.

7.3The Gomoku notation must be visible to the referee during the whole game.

7.4The tournament rules may set a time limit after which the player is not required to keep his/her annotation updated. A player is however, required to complete the annotation of all the Moves made, after the end of the match.

7.5A player may, in the case of the occurrence of the event described in the article 7.4, ask one of the spectators to write the Moves down for him/her.

7.6After the game has ended, players will write down the final result of the game, the number of the last Move and the remaining time of both players. By signing the Gomoku notations, they confirm the accuracy of the information on both Gomoku notations.

Article 8 — Draw

8.1A player may offer a draw after executing his/her Move. However, he/she must do this before pressing his/her clock.

8.2The offer of a draw cannot be withdrawn. The offer stands until the opponent has accepted it, rejected it verbally, rejected it by executing a Move, or the game has ended in another way.

Article 9 — Scoring

9.1A player is rewarded one point (1) for winning a game or for winning by default, zero points (0) for losing a game, or losing by default, and a half a point (0.5) for a draw.

Article 10 — Behaviour of Players

10.1Players must act in a way which will not harm the good name of Gomoku.

10.2“The Game place” is considered “the Game room”, the room for relaxation and refreshments, toilets, smoking places and places intended for other purposes, set by the referee.

10.3The Game room is considered the room where competitive games take place.

10.4Only with permission from the referee may a player:

10.4.1leave the Game place.

10.4.2leave the Game room, given that it is his/her turn to execute a Move.

10.5Tournament rules may specify that a player whose time is not running must notify the referee in case he/she would like to leave the Game room.

10.6Players are forbidden to use any kind of notes, information sources and advice. Players are also not allowed to analyse any game on any other Board.

10.7The tournament rules may restrict or forbid the use of electronic devices during a game.

10.8Players who have finished their game are considered spectators.

10.9It is forbidden to disrupt or annoy an opponent in any way, including making noise in the Game room.

10.10Breaking rules which are discussed in articles 10.1 to 10.9 will be punished in accordance with the article 11.9.

10.11A player will be punished by losing the game in the case where he/she repeatedly disobeys the rules. In the case where both players are to blame, the referee will state that the game has been lost by both players.

10.12A player has the right to ask a referee to explain a certain rule/article from the rules.

10.13Both players must help the referee in any situation where it is necessary to reconstruct a position.

Article 11 — The Role of the Referee (see Foreword)

11.1The referee will oversee compliance with these rules.

11.2The referee:

11.2.1oversees the compliance with the rules.

11.2.2acts in the best interests of the tournament.

11.2.3takes care of the good environment in which games take place.

11.2.4ensures that players are not being distracted during a game.

11.2.5oversees the course of the tournament.

11.3The referee will watch the course of the games, especially when there is lack of time. He/she will assert his/her decisions and award the players with sensible punishments.

11.4The referee may name assistants who will help him/her to watch the games, for example, when more players have a lack of remaining time on the clock.

11.5In the case that identical positions occur on multiple Boards and there is a possibility that players could copy Moves from one another, the referee will introduce necessary steps in order to prevent this.

11.6In the case that there is an external interruption of the game, a referee may add extra time to one, or both players.

11.7A referee may not interfere in the game with the exception of the situations which are described in the rules of the competition. A referee may not notify a player, that his/her opponent has executed his/her Move or that he/she has forgotten to press the clock.

11.8In the event that someone discovers an impropriety, the referee is the sole person that should be notified. Players engaged in other games may not discuss a game and or interfere with it in any way. The spectators are not allowed to interfere in a game, with the exception of the situation described in article 7.5. In the event that it is necessary, the referee may eject the offenders from the Game place.

11.9The referee has the right to award these punishments:

  • 11.9.1 Warning.
  • 11.9.2 Adding time to the offender’s opponent.
  • 11.9.3 Subtracting time from the offender.
  • 11.9.4 Declaring the game as a “lost game” for the offender.
  • 11.9.5 A fine defined in advance.
  • 11.9.6 Ejection from one or more rounds.
  • 11.9.7 Ejection from the tournament.

Article 12 — Official international rating

Introduced by the amendment of the Gomoku rules valid from 1/10/2026.

12.1An official international Gomoku rating system is introduced by RIF Gomoku Committee in order to encourage competition within the sport and distinguish the level of players. This rating system shall apply to all tournaments previously conducted under these rules, including those held before this amendment entered into force.

12.2Within the Gomoku rating system, there are two separate rating lists introduced: Gomoku Rating and Gomoku Blitz Rating. They are distinguished by the time control of the games, respectively the Uniform Time Control (UTC), which is calculated in accordance with the following formula:

Uniform Time Control UTC = basic time (per player) + extra time per move (Fischer) × 30

12.2.1Gomoku Rating is used for games with the time control of no less than 20 minutes per player (or following 1/10/2026 also its equivalent UTC, providing that basic time is no less than 15 minutes).

12.2.2Gomoku Blitz Rating is used for games with the time control of no less than 2 minutes and less than 20 minutes (or following 1/10/2026 also its equivalent UTC).

12.3The Whole-History Rating (WHR) is used with the same parameters as for the RIF International Renju Rating.

12.4Tournaments that meet the following conditions will be counted in the rating list:

12.4.1The tournament needs to be played according to these Gomoku rules (until year 2008 also games played by swap1 rule can be counted).

12.4.2The complete game results of the tournament must be entered into the database on the RIF website. In case of Gomoku Rating, games must be published on the RIF website with all required information, including a full record of the moves, recorded in accordance with Article 9 of these rules. For tournaments played before this amendment (up to 1/10/2026), the full record of the moves is not required. The record of the moves is not required for Gomoku Blitz Rating.

12.4.3The tournament needs to be played live, face-to-face. The tournament needs to be organized or authorized by the respective national association or by RIF.

12.4.4If it turns out that the conditions of these rules were breached during a tournament, so that the tournament formally no longer meets the requirements for inclusion in the rating list, the Gomoku Committee, having examined the circumstances of the tournament, may by a majority vote of all its members decide to keep the tournament (in whole or in part) in the rating list.

12.5About active and established players:

12.5.1A player is an active established player, if he/she played at least 10 rated games, and the last game was played no more than 5 years ago.

12.5.2A player is an inactive established player, if he/she played at least 10 rated games, and the last game was played more than 5 years ago.

12.5.3A player is a provisional player, if he/she played no more than 9 rated games.

12.6The rating list is updated automatically every day with the open source code.
